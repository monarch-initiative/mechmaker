</figcaption>
</figure></div><p>FLINT, Mich. (WJRT) - Health officials urge no contact with the Flint River this week after the Flint Water Pollution Control Facility discharged a mix of stormwater and partially treated sewage into the waterway over the weekend.</p><p>The discharge began during a rainfall event at 8:20 a.m. Saturday, which exceeded the plant's capacity for full secondary treatment. More than 11 million gallons of untreated sewage entered the river over about 32 hours until 5 p.m. Sunday.</p><div></div><p>The flow of wastewater, which was heavily diluted by stormwater, into the facility filled the retention and treatment basin ahead of the treatment facility. The plant adds a partial disinfectant before sending untreated water into the river.</p><p>The Genesee County Health Department advises against any contact with the Flint River downstream of the city's wastewater facility due to potentially high bacterial levels. That includes areas of Flint Township, Flushing and Montrose.</p><p>Water sample test results will be made available on the State of Michigan website.</p><div></div><p>The sewage release came nearly a month after Flint City Council members approved a $142,000 project to repair a sluice gate, which controls the wastewater flow into the treatment plant.</p><div><div>
